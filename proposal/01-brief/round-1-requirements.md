# Round 1 requirements and judging

Sources: [Repository problem statement](../../Problem%20Statement/NEXUS-2026-UIUX-Round-1-Problem-Statement.pdf), [official UI/UX handbook](https://nexus.rotaractmora.org/handbooks/nexus-2026-ui-ux-handbook.pdf), [official track calendar](https://nexus.rotaractmora.org/ui-ux).

## What the organizers expect

Design an AI-powered, gamified way for food donors, volunteers and communities in need to coordinate redistribution. Pick a specific situation, show the underlying causes of friction and explain a distinctive experience the team can prototype. Research all three groups before settling on the primary user.

1. Identify pain points and root causes using interviews, surveys, observation, articles or clearly labelled assumptions.
2. Pick a target audience and build one persona per group with goals, frustrations, context and device and data access.
3. Define the core experience, its audience and what makes it different from apps and food-sharing groups.
4. Explain each AI capability, its inputs, its decision and what appears in the experience. Justify decisions handled by rules.
5. Explain each game mechanic, the behavior it supports and how fairness, honesty and safety are protected.

For each AI capability and game mechanic, give the problem it solves, how it works in one or two sentences, where it appears, and its limitations and risks. Consider accessibility, language, low-end devices, dignity, personal data and food safety. Cite research and declare all third-party tools, AI assistance, imagery, icons and kits used. The handbook expects concepts, journeys, wireframes and prototypes to be the registered team's original work.

## What to submit

| PDF section | Include | Brief |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem statement | Chosen context, affected people, root causes and why it matters, with evidence or explicit assumptions | pp. 5, 9–10 |
| User personas | Donor, volunteer and recipient with context and access limits | pp. 4–5, 10 |
| Key user journey | Need to outcome, actions, emotions, pain points and interventions | p. 10 |
| Solution and key features | Core experience, comparison, AI and gamification rationale | pp. 6–8, 10 |
| Initial sketches or wireframes | Original rough screens for the core flow, with key states | p. 10 |
| References | Research citations and third-party asset and tool declarations | pp. 9–10 |

Submit one PDF via the official portal, named `NEXUS_UIUX_TEAMNAME_R1.pdf`. The brief gives a **proposed 6–8 pages excluding references**. The public track page lists **17–24 November 2026**, with the exact cutoff time and upload URL still to confirm via team communications. The handbook says late submissions will be rejected. Teams are 1–4 members. The top 24 advance.

Round 1 needs rough sketches and a concept. The brief calls for Round 2 to show a high-fidelity interactive prototype with AI matching, donation and collection workflows, missions, points, badges, leaderboards and impact tracking.

## Evidence of a strong entry

| Judging criterion | What the proposal should make easy to see |
| --- | --- |
| Problem understanding | A narrow problem, sourced causes, all three perspectives and honest evidence limits |
| Creativity | A concrete handoff that improves on a recognizable current workflow |
| Innovation | An explainable match and a reward tied to a verified, worthwhile action, with failure states |
| Feasibility | One reproducible end-to-end flow with clear ownership and a manageable prototype scope |

The brief lists these four criteria without scoring weights. The table translates them into review checks for this project.
