# Working problem and assumptions

## Problem to investigate

In a Colombo catchment, a food business may only know that it has eligible surplus close to closing. To redistribute it, someone has to find a receiving partner that can accept the food and a volunteer who can complete the run within the available window. Colombo stakeholder research documented short evening windows, transport constraints and uncertainty about hygiene and responsibility. Failure to coordinate can mean a missed donation or an unreliable meal update. The current frequency and effects on individual recipients are open research questions. [Local study E1](../02-research/sri-lanka-evidence.md)

**Design question:** How might we help a volunteer and a nearby receiving partner make a feasible handoff decision quickly, while giving recipients reliable, private information and an option to decline?

| Group | Supported observation | Working cause or assumption | What to check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Donor | Historical local research reports short release windows and concerns around safety and accountability (E1). | H1. Staff discover availability late and need a quick way to confirm who will accept responsibility and when. | Walk through one real closure. Compare permission to donate, time to prepare, handling and transport as blockers. |
| Volunteer | The historical Colombo study records volunteer time and transport constraints, and reports WhatsApp coordination (E5). | H2. Unclear duration, drop-off readiness and last-minute changes make feasible runs hard to identify. | Ask experienced and occasional volunteers about a completed and declined run. Check if availability, fuel costs, distance or schedule dominates. |
| Recipient | WFP documents broader need, and DCS shows variation in digital literacy (E3–E4). | H3. A trusted update in a workable channel and clear ingredient information matter enough to prevent wasted trips or a declined meal. | Ask adults directly what happened the last time plans changed. Test notification timing and the preferred language, channel and degree of detail. |
| Receiving partner | The Colombo study documents tight institutional receiving windows and handling constraints (E1). | H4. A partner can declare batch capacity and hours in advance and confirm or decline each arrival. | Ask a coordinator to rehearse capacity changes, rejects and who can send the eventual update. |

Keep volunteer coordination as the tentative lead experience while checking these assumptions. If recipient access or donor approval emerges as the more common barrier, change the lead journey. If timing and logistics fail despite clear messages because no receiving capacity exists, narrow the catchment or food category before adding features.
