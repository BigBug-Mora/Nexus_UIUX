# AI and gamification

## Matching and what people see

1. **Rule-based eligibility.** Screen each offer against the partner's approved categories and required information, receiving capacity and hours, donor availability, volunteer availability and carrying capability, and the applicable handling procedure. Use an explicit reason and stop on missing information or poor fit. Human review can resolve ambiguity within the approved procedure.
2. **AI-assisted ranking.** For eligible donor–volunteer–partner combinations, a future model estimates late-handoff risk from route estimates, time of day, remaining time and operational history of comparable handoffs. In Round 2, simulate the ranking using prepared scenarios and prototype the decision. Compare later against a transparent baseline that ranks by schedule slack with a fair tie-breaker when there is enough anonymized outcome data.
3. **Explainable recommendation.** On the volunteer's run card and coordinator's queue, show the estimate, receiving window, practical reasons and who confirms. Illustrative copy: “Suggested because the receiving partner is open and this trip fits your availability. Estimated travel time 18 minutes.” Let volunteers decline and coordinators choose a different eligible match.
4. **Fallback and evaluation.** With sparse history, stale data or low confidence, show the rule-based option and route uncertain cases to the coordinator. Measure missed-window rate, calibration, and distribution of offers across partners and volunteer availability bands. Review whether the model systematically favors central areas or highly active volunteers. Keep household addresses, names and sensitive attributes out of ranking. Eligibility remains human-governed. Model output provides scheduling support and cannot certify food safety.

## Game mechanics

| Mechanic and purpose | How it works and where people see it | Limits and safeguards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Team mission · support reliable coordination | A team progress card counts distinct partner-confirmed handoffs toward a weekly target. | Missed runs and good-faith safety stops carry no penalty. Keep progress optional and omit recipients' identities and exact locations. |
| Points · reinforce verified completion | Credit one fixed contribution per eligible completed run after partner confirmation, displayed in the volunteer's private activity log. Count each assignment once. | No points for speed, volume, extra journeys or bypassing review. Hold disputed credits and correct duplicates. |
| Badge · make good practice visible | A private or opt-in badge recognizes completion of orientation and a verified handoff using the required checks. | Explain criteria and offer accessible orientation. Let participants hide badges. |
| Leaderboard · support shared consistency | The Round 2 concept shows optional team-level standings by capped weeks meeting a proportionate reliability goal. Show shared progress on the same screen. | Use comparable commitments and a minimum activity threshold before displaying standings. Review for small-team disadvantage and avoid recipient ranks, public volunteer failure counts or rewards for posting food volume. |
| Impact feedback · show the outcome | Show partner-confirmed portions and closed handoffs in the receipt and aggregate weekly view. | Count the accepted quantity once. Distinguish activity rewards from impact and leave estimates visibly labelled. |

Test whether rewards support trust and retention or introduce pressure. If interviews show reluctance to be ranked, make shared progress the default and demonstrate the opt-in team leaderboard as an alternate view.
